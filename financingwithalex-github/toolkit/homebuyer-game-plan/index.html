The payment, two ways

Lenders look at both numbers, and each lender and loan type has its own limits. One public example for conventional loans: Fannie Mae's rulebook lists 36% as the usual top for the second number, up to 45% with strong credit and savings, and up to 50% when the loan goes through its automated system. Other loan types use other numbers. Ask your lender which apply to you. Source: Fannie Mae Selling Guide B3-6-02.

A lender may approve you for more than this. That is the most they will lend, not what is comfortable to live with.

      From now until you close: 4 things not to do

      Lenders can check your file again right before closing. Each of these can delay a loan or stop it.

      1. Don't finance a car or furniture.A new monthly payment raises your debt-to-income. If a lender finds new debt before closing, they may have to run your loan again.
      2. Don't change or quit your job.Lenders confirm your job again shortly before closing. On conventional loans sold to Fannie Mae, that check is within 10 business days before signing for people paid a wage or salary. If a change is coming, tell your lender first.
      3. Don't open new credit.New cards and loans can lower your credit score and raise the rate you are offered.
      4. Don't move large amounts of money without a paper trail.Lenders have to document where large deposits came from. Keep every statement. If family is giving you money, tell your lender and ask what paperwork they need.
      Never hide or relabel anything. Tell your lender the truth about your income, your debts, where your money came from and how you will use the home.
